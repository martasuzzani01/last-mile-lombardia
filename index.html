Last Mile

Over the last ten years, the floor area of logistics warehouses in Lombardy grew from 10 to 15.8 million m² (+50%), according to a mapping by LIUC Business School. They're often built on the edges of cities, where land is cheaper and communities have less power to object. Every delivery generates a trip, and the regional logistics system now generates roughly 72,000 vehicles a day, according to Unioncamere Lombardia.

This tool simulates a real route between an existing warehouse and a destination in Lombardy — the route and distance are calculated on the real road network (OpenStreetMap / OSRM) — and shows which logistics hubs, with their sources, fall within the route's radius.

methodology & limits

How the model works, and what it isn't

The route between warehouse and destination is calculated in real time by the public OSRM (Open Source Routing Machine) service on the OpenStreetMap road network, using a standard driving profile (not specific to heavy vehicles: it doesn't account for truck access restrictions, height, or weight). The distance and time shown are those returned by the service, not rough estimates. If the service is unreachable (no connection, maintenance), the site falls back to a straight-line distance × 1.3 and says so explicitly.

The route's CO₂ figure is real distance × 1.0 kg CO₂/km. That factor is calculated from a declared average fuel consumption of 0.33–0.43 litres/km for a 40t articulated truck (source: Webfleet) multiplied by the standard conversion factor of 2.68 kg CO₂ per litre of diesel — an average reference value, not a measurement of the vehicle actually used on a specific delivery.

To find the "hubs in range," every warehouse on the map is projected onto the same area and its minimum distance from the real road route returned by OSRM is calculated: hubs within 15 km fall within the impact radius. The exposure index you see in the table combines only two objective factors — the hub's size category (where known from a public source) and its proximity to the route — precisely so as not to mix heterogeneous data (warehouse floor area, municipal income, the 2011 ISTAT index) into a single score that would give a false impression of precision. The real socioeconomic indicators found for each municipality are shown individually, as context, not as part of the score.

What this tool is NOT

It does not measure the real emissions of a specific trip, it does not use real-time traffic data, and the routing profile is not designed for heavy vehicles. Warehouse coordinates are mostly at municipality level or based on an address known from a news/company source (noted case by case in the "sources" table), not surveyed cadastral coordinates. The ISTAT Social and Material Vulnerability Index used as context dates back to the 2011 census (the latest public update available at municipal level on 8MilaCensus) and is not current as of 2026. This research did not find an Italian scientific study that directly quantifies the link between proximity to a logistics warehouse and air quality: it's presented as an under-explored research area, not as a conclusion this site demonstrates.

For real-world or production use

The public OSRM demo server used here is free to use only for low volumes and with no service guarantees; a site with real traffic would need its own OSRM instance or a service such as OpenRouteService, which offers a driving-hgv profile built specifically for heavy vehicles (requires a free API key — see the project's README). Likewise, municipal air-quality data could be fetched live from the ARPA Lombardia open data portal (Socrata endpoint, see README) instead of the 2024 values embedded statically here.
